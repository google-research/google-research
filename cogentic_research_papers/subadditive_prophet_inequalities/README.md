## Abstract

We study the prophet inequality problem for combinatorial auctions in which n
buyers with independent, monotone subadditive valuations over m indivisible
items arrive one at a time. We focus on static anonymous item prices: the seller
posts a single price for each item before any buyer arrives, and each arriving
buyer purchases a utility-maximizing bundle of the remaining items. Feldman,
Gravin, and Lucier (2015) showed that such prices achieve a 2-approximation to
the expected optimal social welfare for fractionally subadditive (XOS)
valuations and an O(log m)-approximation for subadditive valuations, and
Dütting, Kesselheim, and Lucier (2020) later improved the subadditive guarantee
to O(log log m). The optimal approximation ratio of static anonymous item prices
for subadditive buyers has remained open, including whether a constant factor is
achievable.

We determine this ratio up to a log log log log m factor by improving both the
upper and the lower bound. On the upper-bound side, we show that static
anonymous item prices always achieve an O(log log log m)-approximation. On the
lower-bound side, we construct an instance with only two buyers on which no
static anonymous item prices, even randomized ones, achieve better than an
Ω(log log log m / log log log log m)-approximation. In particular, unlike for
XOS valuations, static anonymous item prices cannot achieve a constant-factor
approximation for subadditive valuations.
